# Tiwas — Dragon Location Address Template

**Status: Advisory content-authoring proposal only.** Sixth worked instance of
**DEC-112 L-004**, and the second creature (after Blood Man) whose body plan is
**fundamentally non-humanoid**, chosen deliberately: Blood Man broke DEC-100 reuse because
it lacks *persistent limbs at all*; Dragon breaks it for the opposite reason — it has
plenty of persistent, well-defined anatomy, just not two-arms-two-legs-one-head-one-torso
anatomy. This tests whether "bespoke Tier-1" (the answer the Blood Man proposal reached)
generalizes past its original amorphous-creature motivation to an ordinary, solidly-limbed
quadruped-with-wings-and-tail body plan.

## 0. Provenance Flag — Read Before the Rest of This Document

**Dragon shares the Goblin proposal's posture exactly, not the weaker Orc posture.**
**DEC-084** names Goblin and Dragon together: an earlier advisory-drafted Goblin *and*
Dragon were both ruled to have no GURPS stat block in the source material, and both were
reclassified as candidate **Tiwa-authored new content**, outside the DEC-077.A conversion
workflow but not rejected. This document sits on that same footing — advisory candidate
input with the same weak-but-real precedent Goblin had, stronger than Orc's total absence
of register history, still far short of the standing Ice Troll and Blood Man carry under
DEC-077.A's actual authorization.

## 1. Working Concept

A large, scaled, winged quadruped: four legs (a foreleg pair and a hindleg pair, distinct
from each other in role), a pair of leathery wings, a long tail, a long neck, and a head
bearing horns, eyes, nostrils (breath-weapon origin), and jaws. Heavily armored dorsal
scales contrast deliberately with a proverbially softer underbelly — the "weak point"
motif is load-bearing for this template's design, not incidental (§4).

## 2. Does DEC-100 Reuse Hold Here? — No, Unambiguously

DEC-100's four zones (Legs/Torso/Arms/Head) describe a bipedal humanoid. A dragon has no
"Arms" in that sense at all — its forelimbs are legs — and has three major anatomical
features (wings, tail, a long neck as its own distinct target) that a four-zone humanoid
split has no slot for. Unlike Ice Troll or Goblin/Orc, there is no plausible argument for
reuse here even with heavy internal reinterpretation. Per **DEC-041(5)**, this template
authors a fully **bespoke six-zone Tier-1 split**:

| Tier-1 Zone | Range | Width | What it represents |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Torso / Body | 1–40 | 40 | Core mass — chest, back, belly; largest single zone by far |
| Neck | 41–50 | 10 | Distinctive, myth-significant target between Torso and Head |
| Head | 51–65 | 15 | Skull, face, jaws, breath-weapon origin |
| Tail | 66–80 | 15 | Long, reach-weapon appendage |
| Wings | 81–92 | 12 | Paired — flight-critical, membrane vs. structural bone |
| Legs / Claws | 93–100 | 8 | Four limbs: foreleg pair + hindleg pair |

This mirrors Blood Man's precedent (a bespoke split still spans the same 1–100 range,
still resolved by the same single-source deterministic mechanism, DEC-112 L-003) while
producing a completely different-shaped tree — confirming that "bespoke Tier-1" is a
general answer for non-reuse cases, not something invented specifically for amorphous
creatures.

*(Both Leg branches saturate at Tier 2 — the zone's 8-number budget leaves no meaningful

**Totals check:** Torso 24+16=40 (Foreback 18+3+1+2=24; Underbelly 10+1+1+1+3=16); Neck
7+3=10; Head 8+7=15 (Face 2+2+3=7); Tail 12+3=15; Wings 8+4=12; Legs 5+3=8. Sum = 100. ✓

### 3.7 Laterality — the first four-limb case

Laterality is still a **single computation from the raw Zero-Step index** (odd = Left,
even = Right), applied uniformly to whichever address it resolves into — identical in
principle to every prior template. What's new here is that the **Legs/Claws zone requires
two independent pieces of information from one index**: which structural branch
(Foreleg vs. Hindleg — an ordinary tree decision, like any other Tier-2 split) **and**
which side (Left vs. Right — the parity flag, computed the same way it always is).
Neither piece of information competes with or overrides the other; they are simply
answering different questions from the same number.

Applies to (parity meaningful): Torso.Foreback.Lung, Head.Face.Eyes, Wings (both nodes —
an entire Tier-1 zone determined by parity, the first such case in this template family),
Legs.Foreleg, Legs.Hindleg. Discarded (midline): Torso.Dorsal Scales/Chest, Heart, Spine
Ridge, Underbelly, Stomach, Liver, Intestines, Hindquarter Hide, Neck, Throat, Skull,
Nostrils, Jaws/Fangs, Tail-Body, Tail-Tip.

*Worked example:* index **95** → `Legs.Foreleg`; 95 is odd → **Left Foreleg**. Index **84**
→ `Wings.Wing-Membrane`; 84 is even → **Right Wing, Membrane**.

## 4. Armor Placement — A Worked Exception-Carving Example

Unlike Goblin/Orc (no innate armor) or Ice Troll (a single environmentally-conditional
Trait covering whole Tier-1 zones), Dragon is proposed to have **permanent, unconditional**
scaled armor — no `env:` Condition Clause is needed (DEC-088's machinery is for
*conditional* Traits; ordinary armor doesn't require it) — but that armor **does not cover
the whole body**, which requires registering `defense:armor` at **Tier-2 nodes**, not the
coarser Tier-1 parents, so that DEC-112 L-007's parent-covers-child rule doesn't
accidentally extend coverage into the deliberately soft zones:

| Node armor is registered at | Covers (via L-007) | Deliberately excluded |
|---|---|---|
| `Torso.Foreback` | Dorsal Scales/Chest, Lung, Heart, Spine Ridge | `Torso.Underbelly` and everything under it — **not** covered, because armor is registered at the Foreback branch, not the Torso Tier-1 parent |
| `Neck` (registered at the Tier-2 "Neck generic" node specifically) | — | `Neck.Throat` — excluded the same way |
| `Head.Skull` | (terminal node, no children) | `Head.Face` and everything under it (Eyes, Nostrils, Jaws) |
| `Tail.Tail-Body` and `Tail.Tail-Tip` | — | (fully armored; no exclusion) |
| `Wings.Wing-Strut` | — | `Wings.Wing-Membrane` — excluded |
| `Legs.Foreleg`, `Legs.Hindleg` | — | (fully armored; no exclusion) |

This is the same **L-007 parent-coverage mechanism** the Ice Troll proposal used, but
deployed at a deliberately *finer* grain specifically to carve the mythic weak points
(Underbelly, Throat, Face) out of an otherwise near-total-coverage creature — a direct,
mechanically load-bearing implementation of "the dragon's weak point" rather than a purely
narrative flourish.

## 5. Breath Weapon and Regeneration — Content Flags Only

- **`Head.Face.Nostrils` is a natural anchor point** for a future breath-weapon-related
  Effect or vulnerability, mirroring how prior templates flagged anchor points (Ice
  Troll's claws, Goblin's hand, Orc's arm). No such Effect is authored or proposed here.
- **No Regeneration/Regrowth content proposed** — unlike Ice Troll, nothing about the
  "dragon" archetype as commonly used inherently implies regeneration; if a specific
  dragon subtype (e.g. an elemental or undead dragon) warrants it, that would be
  content-authored per DEC-110 on top of this template, not a change to it.

## 6. Open Questions for Tiwa's Ruling

1. **Underbelly width — wide (10, current) vs. narrow (2–3, "called-shot only" in
   spirit).** The current design makes the belly a *large* target once a Location Index
   reaches that depth at all, rather than a small precise one — modeling "the weak point is
   being exposed/soft over a wide area" rather than "the weak point is one exact scale."
   This is defensible procedurally: reaching Tier-3 depth already requires the attacker's
   Skill-Tier ≥ 2 (DEC-041(1)) and, for a declared called shot, satisfying the §14.7
   Named-Outcome Test gates — so width mainly matters for *un-called* deep hits, not for
   trivializing a deliberate "aim for the gap in the scales" attempt, which those gates
   already govern regardless of range width. A narrower Underbelly is equally defensible
   if Tiwa wants the belly to stay hard-to-hit even at high Skill-Tier.
2. **Size tag.** DEC-080's alpha Tag list (`creature:size_small/medium/large`) has no
   category obviously large enough for a dragon in the traditional sense; this document
   proposes `size_large` provisionally but flags that a `size_huge` or `size_gargantuan`
   extension may be warranted (a Tag-vocabulary extension, not something this document can
   authorize — DEC-080 T1 permits future extension without base-model change, but the
   specific new Tag entry itself needs its own ruling).
3. **Armor exclusion granularity (§4).** Registering armor at Tier-2 (Foreback, not
   Torso) works cleanly here because Torso happens to split exactly along the
   armored/unarmored boundary. A creature whose weak point didn't align so neatly with an
   existing Tier-2 boundary would need either a deeper registration or a restructured
   tree — worth confirming this pattern (register armor as deep as needed to exclude the
   weak point, per L-007) is the intended general approach for future templates, not just
   a coincidence of this one's shape.
4. **Jaws/Fangs left unarmored (§4)** is a simplification — a dragon's teeth are not
   realistically "soft," but the whole `Head.Face` branch was excluded from Skull's armor
   registration for simplicity. A finer design could re-armor Jaws specifically while
   leaving Eyes/Nostrils exposed; not done here to keep the tree's exception list simple.

## Appendix B — Six-Template Comparison

| Feature | Human | Ice Troll | Blood Man | Goblin | Orc | Dragon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier-1 source | DEC-100 | DEC-100 (reused) | Bespoke | DEC-100 (reused) | DEC-100 (reused) | **Bespoke** |
| Number of Tier-1 zones | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | **6** |
| Body plan class | Biped | Biped | Amorphous | Biped | Biped | **Quadruped + wings + tail** |
| Innate Armor Tag | None | Whole zones, freezing-gated | None | None | None | **Sub-zone-granular, permanent, deliberately incomplete** |
| Paired Tier-1 zone | No | No | No | No | No | **Yes (Wings)** |
| Limbs of the same type sharing one zone | No (Arms/Legs split) | No | n/a | No | No | **Yes (4 legs, one zone)** |
| Authorization posture | Own standing | DEC-077.A | DEC-077.A | Weak (DEC-084) | None | **Weak (DEC-084, same as Goblin)** |

**What Dragon confirms:** "bespoke Tier-1" is not a special case invented to handle
amorphous creatures — it is the general answer whenever a creature's body plan doesn't
match DEC-100's specific bipedal assumptions, for *any* reason, including the very
ordinary reason of "this is a normal, solidly-limbed animal that just isn't shaped like a
person." Six templates in, the dividing line is exactly what Appendix B of the Blood Man
proposal predicted: not size, not monstrousness, not even limb count — specifically
whether the creature's silhouette is a stable-limbed **biped**.
