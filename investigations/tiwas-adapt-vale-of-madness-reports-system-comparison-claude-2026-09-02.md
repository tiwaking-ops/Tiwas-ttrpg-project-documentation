## 1. Status and Purpose

This document is:

- **NON-CANONICAL** — advisory only, per the Tiwas governance model (`AGENTS.md`, `archive/README.md`, `governance/status-model.md` as reflected in the supplied corpus).
- **Complementary** to any existing priority-tier / disagreement-register compilation of the same 25 reports; it does not reproduce that compilation and should be read alongside it, not instead of it.
- **System-indexed rather than priority-indexed** — organized by Tiwas mechanical system/subsystem so a reader can inspect one area at a time.
- **Non-ruling, non-promotional, non-authoritative** — it changes nothing about the status of any Tiwas material.

**Agreement among reporting LLMs does not create a Tiwas ruling.** Where multiple reports independently reach the same classification, that is recorded as *convergence* — a documentary observation about the reports — never as evidence sufficient to promote, lock, or otherwise change the status of any Tiwas system. All rulings remain Tiwa's alone.

## 2. Basis and Method

### 2.1 Source boundary

Three sources were consulted, exactly as supplied:

1. `tiwas-adapt-vale-of-madness-reports-1-llm-attribution-2026-09-02.md` — the 25-report corpus and its attribution manifest.
2. `Tiwas-Alpha-Playtest-Corpus-2026-09-01.md` — the Tiwas baseline (Canonical/Locked Part A, Ruled non-canonical Part B, explicitly-not-table-ready Part C).
3. `Beyond-the-Vale-of-Madness-GURPS.md` — the external adventure benchmark (an OCR extract of the source PDF; several picture-text blocks in this file are illegible/decorative scans and contain no extractable mechanical content).

No existing priority/disagreement companion document was supplied as a permitted source for this task and none was used or reproduced.

### 2.2 Attribution method

Author identity for each of the 25 reports was taken directly from the attribution manifest and its confirmed corrections (the repaired, de-duplicated Copilot / Copilot 365 pairs; the Muse Spark 1.1 and Mistral confirmations dated 2026‑09‑02), not inferred from writing style. Report numbering (1 or 2 per LLM) follows the manifest's "Report no." column. Where the manifest itself records uncertainty or a correction history, that uncertainty is preserved rather than smoothed over (see Report 24's self-identification as "claude sonnet-5" in its own front matter, versus manifest-assigned attribution for every other report).

### 2.3 Report-level evidence discipline

Each report's claims were read against three separate registers and kept distinct throughout this compilation:

- **Tiwas Canonical/Locked material** (Part A of the corpus — e.g. the Core Test Transaction, the 24-attribute matrix, Zero-Step).
- **Tiwas Non-canonical designer rulings** (Part B — e.g. S-3 through S-11 as recorded 2026‑09‑01).
- **Reports' own classifications, recommendations, and inferences** — including cases where a report's own numeric summary does not exactly match its own per-item table (flagged, not corrected).

GURPS mechanics described in the adventure are never treated as Tiwas authority in this document, even when a reporting LLM's phrasing blurs that line.

### 2.4 Comparison method

Reports were grouped by the Tiwas system, subsystem, or explicitly-named mechanical area each discussed. A system is included here if at least one report names it as a distinct requirement, gap, or ruled subsystem — including missing, open, and design-stage systems, per the task's instruction not to restrict coverage to already-canonical material.

### 2.5 Disagreement method

A **genuine disagreement** was recorded only where two or more reports reach *materially different classifications or conclusions about the same Tiwas system*, not merely different wording for the same conclusion. Reports that use different vocabulary but converge on the same substantive classification (e.g. "Playtestable Now" vs. "Ready" vs. "🟢") are treated as agreement. Reports that address different aspects of a system without contradicting one another (e.g. one covers the base mechanic, another covers a specific numeric gap within it) are recorded as **complementary observations**, not disagreement.

## System 4: Skill/Attribute Defaults (Untrained Fallback)

### Status / Tiwas baseline
No mechanism in either Part A or Part B of the supplied Tiwas corpus permits a Skill test to be attempted using a substitute attribute or unrelated Skill at a penalty when the named Skill is absent or at 0.

### Reporting LLMs

| LLM | Report(s) | Classification |
|---|---:|---|
| Kimi K3 | 1 | Design-Stage Dependency — **Critical** |
| Claude 5 Sonnet | 1 | Missing Subsystem — moderate/high blocking |
| Perplexity | 1 | Design-Stage Dependency |

### Per-report positions

**[Kimi K3 — Report 1]** — Position: this is one of three critical rulings blocking full-fidelity combat/skill resolution. Quote: *"Eight adventure calls require attempting a skill without training. Tiwas has no default mechanism. A Skill‑0 test can never succeed (d100 range 1–100, success requires Roll ≤ 0 is impossible)."* Kimi's priority list places this alongside damage quantification and defense-skill specification as the three rulings needed before a full playtest.

**[Claude 5 Sonnet — Report 1]** — Position: independently identifies the same gap by the same reasoning, and further finds it sits on the adventure's ice-cave *main path*, not an optional branch. Quote: *"No Tiwas mechanism exists for testing an attribute/related-skill in place of an absent Skill... M3 sits on the ice‑cave main path... the Missing Subsystem status of M3 is not an edge case."*

**[Perplexity — Report 1]** — Position: treats the *fallback itself* as uncertain rather than flatly missing, citing it as an unconfirmed procedure rather than asserting its complete absence. Quote: *"Tiwas has attributes and Tier‑1 skills, but the exact policy for an untrained attribute‑based fallback must be confirmed... classified as Playtestable with substitution — assumption."*

### Collated synthesis

> **Agreement:** two independently-run reports (Kimi K3 and Claude 5 Sonnet) converge — without apparent knowledge of one another's work — on identifying the same specific mechanical gap, using near-identical reasoning about Skill-0 and d100's 1–100 range, and both flag it among the highest-priority blockers they found. This is a strong instance of unprompted multi-LLM convergence on a gap not otherwise discussed at this level of precision by the remaining 23 reports.
>
> **Unresolved:** whether this should be classified Missing Subsystem (Claude 5 Sonnet, and Kimi K3's own severity framing) or a milder Design-Stage/substitutable gap (Perplexity's more provisional framing).
>
> **Coverage limitation:** this specific, named gap is **not discussed at all** by the other 22 reports, which either do not reach this level of granularity or fold "untrained checks" into general skill-mapping discussion without separately flagging the default-substitution mechanism as absent.
