# Tiwas — Next S-2 Design Question: Non-Attack Location Index Source (Starter Brief v1.1)

**Use this to open a new chat.** Continues the S-2 Design Investigation series (v1–v5) after the invocation/warrant
policy was accepted as a non-canonical candidate, and after a second correction round reclassified Structural
Weak Points from State 1 to State 2. This is a distinct, narrower question flagged repeatedly during the
original investigation but deliberately not resolved there.

---

## 1. What's already settled (don't re-litigate)

- **Zero-Step (Tier-1 Location Index provider):** Canonical / Locked. Canonical Rules §14.1–§14.2. Not open
  for reconsideration.
- **S-1 opposed contest:** Canonical / Locked. Not open for reconsideration.
- **S-2 invocation/warrant policy:** Accepted as the current **non-canonical candidate** (S-2 Design
  Investigation v1–v5). Governs *when a Location Index is warranted for a given resolution* via a four-state
  model (Established & Resolvable / Established, Not Yet Resolvable / Outcome Plausible but
  Location-Dependence Unresolved / No Distinct Consequence), a Named-Outcome Test for declared objectives,
  and a set of confirmed procedural rules (disjunctive compound objectives, stale-objective invalidation,
  S-1 winner-only eligibility, lazy evaluation — this one a **design inference**, not an empirically
  replicated finding like the other three — and explicit-only objectives, a **designer ruling**, not just a
  tested finding).
- **Structural Weak Points = State 2 (Established, Not Yet Resolvable)**, not State 1 — a designer ruling
  made in a second correction round after the original v1–v5 investigation closed. Location-dependence is
  anchored (no competing non-location mechanism is proposed for it, unlike Disarm/Armor Bypass/etc.), but
  no current rule provides a mechanism — a Location Index→structural-component mapping — to act on the
  result. Anchored ≠ Resolvable.
- **W3 cache:** demoted to a reference/shortcut layer for situations already established by the policy
  above. It does not independently authorize generation. **Current cache contains zero State-1 (Active/
  Generate) entries.** Structural Weak Points sits at State 2 (established, pending a resolution mechanism);
  Disarm, Equipment Damage, Function Impairment, Armor Bypass, and Incapacitation all sit at State 3 —
  plausible outcomes whose *location-dependence itself* is an open design fork, not a resolvability gap. Do
  not re-promote these without a new subsystem (S-3/S-5/S-7/S-10) making an explicit location-is-the-mechanism
  choice, and do not treat Structural Weak Points as Active/Generate without a settled mapping mechanism.
- **Scene/campaign tier policy (Tier 0/1/2 selection):** explicitly **not** resolved by the invocation
  policy above, despite some earlier drafting drift toward implying otherwise — that drift was caught and
  corrected in the governance documents. Still fully open.

None of this is open for reconsideration here. Treat the corrected v5 synthesis (attached/uploaded) as the
authoritative record of what was established and how — including the second correction round.

## 2. The question

> **Zero-Step derives a Location Index from "the attacker's natural d100 roll." Non-attack physical
> resolutions — a collapsing bridge, a fall, a structural accident, an environmental hazard with no
> adversarial party — have no "attacker" in that sense. Whose roll, if any, supplies the Location Index
> for these cases, and does the existing invocation policy even apply to them in its current form?**

This surfaced independently at least twice during the prior investigation (a rope-bridge fall scenario, used
in both blind table-practice tests) and was deliberately kept out of scope both times because it's a
distinct architectural question, not a warrant-boundary question.

## 3. Do this before generating candidate answers

- Establish what "the roll" even means in a resolution with no attacker. Candidates to test, not assume:
  a test made by the affected character (e.g., a Reflexes/Agility-type Skill test to react to the hazard);
  a roll generated by the hazard/GM directly with no character Skill test underlying it at all; reuse of
  whatever Core Test already resolves the hazard's effect (if one exists) as the roll Zero-Step consumes;
  or some hazards simply falling outside Location Index scope entirely by design, not by omission.
- Check whether the invocation policy's four-state model and Named-Outcome Test transfer cleanly to
  non-attack cases, or whether "declared objective" (which presumes an acting party with intent) needs a
  non-attack-appropriate reformulation (a hazard doesn't "declare an objective" the way a player does).
- Confirm this doesn't quietly reopen Zero-Step itself — the derivation mechanism (digit exchange) is not
  in question; only *whose roll* feeds it is.

## 4. Explicit scope guards

- Do not reopen Zero-Step's derivation mechanism, S-1, or the accepted invocation/warrant policy.
- Do not reopen the Structural Weak Points State-2 ruling, or any other State classification — those are
  settled for this investigation's purposes, even though S-4 wound activation, Tier-2, and the anatomical
  mapping question remain separately open elsewhere.
- Do not redesign the W3 cache or revisit State-3 entries — those wait on S-3/S-5/S-7/S-10, unrelated to
  this question.
- Do not touch Tier 0/1/2 scene-selection policy — still separately open, not this investigation's job.
- Do not design anatomical mapping, Tier-2 mechanics, or Wound/Armor/Effect numerical rules.

## 5. Suggested process

Given how the prior S-2 investigation went, the same discipline is worth reusing: state a candidate answer
and its reasoning, then stress-test it against concrete scenarios (a fall, a collapsing structure, a
generic environmental hazard, a hazard the character caused vs. one that just happens to them, a hazard
resolved via an existing Core Test vs. one with no clear underlying test at all) before treating any answer
as more than a candidate. A blind table-practice round, in the same format used for the warrant investigation,
is likely to be useful here too, given how effective it was at catching hidden assumptions last time — but
don't assume that in advance; decide once the candidate answer is drafted.
